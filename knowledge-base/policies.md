# AnyCompany Energy — Customer Policies

*Part of AnyCompany Utilities. This document sets out AnyCompany Energy's policies on payment
arrangements, arrears and disconnection, vulnerable-customer protections and the Priority
Services Register, meter-reading cadence, and the complaints and Ombudsman escalation path.
It is the authoritative source agents should cite when discussing these topics. All figures
are illustrative for the 2026/27 charging year and shown in GBP.*

> **Consistency note.** These policies align with `billing-faqs.md`, `tariff-guide.md` and
> `service-procedures.md`. Where a customer needs an appointment, agents use the appointment
> types defined in `service-procedures.md` (METER_READING, ENGINEER_VISIT,
> LEAK_INVESTIGATION, EV_CHARGER_INSTALL).

## 1. Payment plans and payment arrangements

We want every customer to be able to pay in a way that works for their circumstances. If you
are worried about paying, contact us **early** — we have several options.

### 1.1 Payment arrangement (short-term)
A short-term arrangement spreads a single overdue amount across a small number of agreed
instalments (typically up to **3 months**) on top of your ongoing usage charges. Suitable for
a one-off shortfall, such as a catch-up bill after estimated reads.

### 1.2 Payment plan (longer-term)
A formal payment plan spreads arrears over a longer period (typically **6 to 24 months**)
based on what you can realistically afford, established through an **income-and-expenditure
review**. We will:
- Agree affordable instalments covering arrears **plus** ongoing usage.
- Confirm the plan in writing, including the amount, frequency and review date.
- **Suspend collections and disconnection activity** while the plan is being honoured.
- Review the plan if your circumstances change.

> A customer on a payment plan must not be defaulted for a single failed payment without the
> collections team first attempting contact and reviewing the plan. Example: *James Okafor
> (ACC-100456)* is on a payment plan — a bounced Direct Debit should trigger a plan review,
> not an immediate default (see `billing-faqs.md` Q13).

### 1.3 Other support options
- **Direct Debit smoothing** — spread annual cost evenly across 12 months (see
  `billing-faqs.md` Q10).
- **Payment breaks** — a temporary pause in limited hardship cases, by agreement.
- **Energy/water efficiency advice** — to reduce ongoing consumption and bills.
- **Trust fund / hardship grants** — referrals to the AnyCompany Utilities customer assistance fund
  and external charities for customers in severe financial difficulty.
- **Benefit-entitlement and debt-advice referrals** — to free, independent services.

### 1.4 How to request help
Contact us by phone, app, or online before your due date. We will discuss your situation
confidentially and recommend the most appropriate option. Asking for help does not affect
your supply.

## 2. Arrears and disconnection policy

Disconnection is always a **last resort**. We follow a structured, fair process and give you
every opportunity to resolve arrears first.

### 2.1 Arrears stages
1. **Reminder.** A friendly reminder shortly after a missed payment, inviting you to pay or
   get in touch.
2. **Second notice.** A formal notice of the overdue amount with clear options to pay or set
   up an arrangement.
3. **Pre-action contact.** Direct attempts to contact you (phone, letter, app, email) to
   agree a payment plan and signpost support before any further action.
4. **Formal arrears / collections.** If contact and arrangements are unsuccessful, the account
   moves to collections. We will still offer a payment plan at this stage.

An account is flagged **ARREARS** once it has a significant overdue balance past its due date
and no active arrangement. Example: *Priya Patel (ACC-100789)* is in arrears (status ARREARS)
**and** is on the Priority Services Register, so the vulnerable-customer protections in
Section 3 apply and disconnection is not pursued.

### 2.2 Before any disconnection, we will always:
- Attempt to contact you through multiple channels and clearly explain the consequences.
- Offer an affordable payment plan and assess affordability.
- Check whether you are on, or eligible for, the **Priority Services Register** (Section 3).
- Offer a prepayment-meter installation as an alternative to disconnection, where safe and
  practical.
- Provide written notice with a minimum **7 working days'** opportunity to respond before any
  installation of a prepayment meter or further action.

### 2.3 We will NOT disconnect (vulnerable-customer protections)
We will **not** disconnect a domestic customer for debt where:
- The customer or a household member is on the **Priority Services Register** or is otherwise
  identified as vulnerable (Section 3).
- It is during the **winter moratorium period (1 October to 31 March)** for households with
  vulnerable occupants, including those with children under defined ages, pensioners, or
  people with disabilities or long-term health conditions.
- The debt is in genuine dispute (see Section 5) — the disputed amount is not pursued while
  open.

For **water supply** specifically, disconnection of a household for non-payment is not
permitted under sector rules; we use payment plans, support, and (only as a last resort)
managed metering instead.

### 2.4 Reconnection
Where supply has been interrupted and the underlying issue is resolved (payment plan agreed
or debt cleared), we reconnect promptly and waive reconnection charges for vulnerable
customers.

## 3. Vulnerable customers and the Priority Services Register (PSR)

The **Priority Services Register (PSR)** is a free service for customers who need extra
support. Registration is voluntary, confidential, and can be done by the customer or, with
consent, by someone on their behalf.

### 3.1 Who can register
Customers (or household members) who:
- Are of pensionable age, or have children under 5.
- Have a disability or long-term health condition.
- Rely on electricity or water for medical equipment (e.g. dialysis, oxygen, stairlifts).
- Have a mental-health condition, learning difficulty, or are recovering from a serious
  illness.
- Have a temporary need (e.g. recent bereavement, recovering from hospital, new baby).
- Have communication needs (sight, hearing, language).

### 3.2 What PSR customers receive
- **Priority support** during supply interruptions, including advance notice of planned work.
- **Protection from disconnection** for debt (Section 2.3).
- **Tailored communication** — large print, braille, audio, or a nominated representative.
- **Identification and meter-reading help** — including a password/quote scheme so you can
  verify a genuine AnyCompany engineer at the door.
- **Bottled-water or alternative-supply arrangements** prioritised during water-supply
  interruptions.
- **Proactive welfare checks** during prolonged outages or extreme weather.

### 3.3 How agents must handle vulnerable customers
When a customer is flagged vulnerable or on the PSR (for example *Priya Patel, ACC-100789*),
agents must:
- Treat any service or billing issue with elevated priority.
- Apply the no-disconnection protection automatically.
- Offer an affordable payment plan and signpost support (Section 1).
- Where appropriate, raise an escalation **ticket** with category **VULNERABILITY**, which
  carries the shortest SLA (priority handling). See the escalation contract in the project
  documentation and Section 5 below.

## 4. Meter-reading schedule and cadence

Accurate reads keep bills correct and avoid estimates (see `billing-faqs.md` Section 3).

| Meter type | Read source | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Smart meter | Automatic remote read | Half-hourly, daily, or monthly per customer preference; minimum monthly for billing |
| Two-rate (Economy 7) | Customer + periodic reader visit | Customer encouraged to submit both registers each quarter; reader visit at least annually |
| Standard / single-rate | Customer + periodic reader visit | Customer encouraged to submit quarterly; reader visit at least annually |

- We aim to obtain at least one **actual** read every **12 months** for safety and accuracy.
- For non-smart meters we attempt a reader visit roughly **quarterly**, and bill on customer
  reads in between.
- Where access is repeatedly unavailable, we will leave a card and may book a **METER_READING**
  appointment (see `service-procedures.md`).
- A sudden, unexplained change in reads may indicate a faulty meter or a leak; for water this
  triggers a **LEAK_INVESTIGATION** (see `service-procedures.md`).

## 5. Complaints, disputes and the Ombudsman escalation path

We aim to resolve concerns quickly and fairly. The following is our escalation ladder.

### 5.1 Stage 1 — Frontline resolution
Raise your concern with us by phone, app, or online. Most issues are resolved at first
contact. If we cannot resolve it immediately, we open a **complaint** (escalation ticket,
category **COMPLAINT**) and give you a reference number. A **billing dispute** is logged as
category **BILLING_DISPUTE**, and the disputed amount is not pursued while open. A supply
problem (e.g. no water, low pressure, leak) is logged as **SUPPLY_ISSUE**.

### 5.2 Stage 2 — Formal review / transfer to a specialist
If you are not satisfied, your complaint is escalated to a senior resolution specialist (a
**transfer to agent** / specialist team handoff). We aim to acknowledge within **2 working
days** and provide a full response in line with the SLA for the complaint's priority:

| Category / priority | Target SLA |
|---|---|
| VULNERABILITY or CRITICAL | 2 hours |
| HIGH | 8 hours |
| MEDIUM (default) | 24 hours |
| LOW | 72 hours |

A ticket raised with category **VULNERABILITY always receives the 2-hour SLA regardless of
the priority supplied** — e.g. a VULNERABILITY ticket logged at `HIGH` priority is still
handled to the 2-hour target, not the 8-hour HIGH target. For all other categories the SLA
follows the priority in the table above.

### 5.3 Stage 3 — Deadlock / "8-week" letter
If we cannot reach an agreement, or **8 weeks** pass without resolution, we issue a
**deadlock letter**. This confirms you may take your complaint to the independent Ombudsman.

### 5.4 Stage 4 — Independent Ombudsman
You may refer an unresolved complaint to the relevant independent redress scheme free of
charge:
- **Energy** matters — the Energy Ombudsman.
- **Water** matters — the Consumer Council for Water (CCW) and, where applicable, the
  redress scheme for water complaints.

The Ombudsman's decision is binding on AnyCompany Energy if you accept it. We will comply with
any remedy ordered, which may include an apology, corrective action, or a goodwill payment.

### 5.5 Agent guidance for escalations
- Always capture an accurate **issue summary** and assign the correct **category**
  (BILLING_DISPUTE, SUPPLY_ISSUE, COMPLAINT, VULNERABILITY, OTHER).
- Set **priority** appropriately (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, CRITICAL); vulnerability and emergencies
  (no water, major leak, customer at risk) warrant CRITICAL.
- For anything involving a vulnerable customer, a safety risk, a legal threat, or an explicit
  request for a manager or the Ombudsman, raise a ticket and transfer to the appropriate
  specialist team without delay.
